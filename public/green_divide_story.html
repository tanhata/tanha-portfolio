TheGreen Divide%

How park investment, quality, and health outcomes split along borough lines.

New York City maintains over 30,000 acres of parkland across five boroughs. But the distribution of capital investment in those parks — and the downstream effects on quality, heat exposure, and respiratory health — varies enormously depending on where you live.

This analysis uses five years of NYC Parks Department budgets, Department of Health hospitalization records, and USGS Landsat thermal satellite imagery to measure the disparity.

Four metrics. One pattern.

Regression analysis yields consistent correlations: each additional $100M in park canopy investment corresponds with a 1.1°F reduction in heat island effect and a 3.2-point improvement in community health scores.

Asthma hospitalization in low-canopy neighborhoods runs 2.3x higher than in high-canopy areas.

Modeling a 40% needs-weighted redistribution of annual capital budgets projects measurable improvements: the Bronx quality index rising from 52 to 70 by 2028, a 3.2°F heat reduction in target areas, a 34% decrease in asthma hospitalizations, and over 1,200 local green jobs created through a community stewardship model.

The disparity is large, structural, and measurable in health outcomes. The data also shows the path forward.
